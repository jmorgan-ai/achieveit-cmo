# Brand voice and content review checklist

AchieveIt writes plainly and practically for people who already work in government, education and healthcare: problem first,
specific, with a point of view.

## Brand basics
- Positioning: "AchieveIt helps organizations close the gap between strategy and execution so they can actually achieve their plans."
  Tagline: "Let's actually do this."
- Messaging pillars: Visibility (all plans, projects and metrics in one place), Alignment (everyone working toward the same goals),
  Execution (bridge the gap between planning and doing).
- Say "strategy execution", never "strategic execution". For public entities, talk about outcomes they can defend in public, never
  commercial revenue language. Keep internal terms ("SLED", "Sub Market") out of external copy.
- Visual: Poppins (Light 300 body, Regular 400 UI, Semibold 600 headings); green #1caf7b, blue #006697, gray #666666; orange
  #f6b267 reserved for demo CTAs.
- Name a customer only if they're already public (public dashboards page or a published customer story).
- Gating: ungate educational content; gate only proprietary research or buying-intent tools. When in doubt, ungate.

## Checklist (every draft passes before it reaches Amanda)
- The core argument appears in the first third.
- Written for practitioners: every fact answers "so what, for this reader?" No textbook definitions.
- One worked example beats three parallel generalizations.
- One idea per section; no section announces the next; the CTA doesn't recap the article.
- Every statistic traced to its primary source, with year and exact wording logged. Nothing from an AI summary or memory.
- Links on descriptive words mid-sentence, used once, pointing at the primary source or the customer page.
- No AI tells: no self-narration, no repeated "not X, but Y", no reflexive lists of three; vary sentence and paragraph length.
- Includes something only AchieveIt would know: a customer moment, an implementation observation, a real quote.
- Graphics agree with the text and with each other.

Source: Feedback for Cristina: Who Makes Policy in City Government (2026-10-23); edited post "When Leadership Changes, Does Your
City's Strategic Plan Survive?" is the model.
